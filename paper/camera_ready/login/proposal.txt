Subject: Article proposal: Where Did the Injection Stop? Canary Tokens for Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines

Topic
  How to find out where a prompt injection stops inside a multi-agent LLM pipeline, rather
  than only whether it succeeded. The method plants a unique canary token in every injected
  payload and logs the furthest of four checkpoints it reaches: exposed in a tool result,
  persisted to shared memory, relayed to a second agent, or executed in an outbound call.
  The article is a shortened, practitioner-oriented version of our paper "Kill-Chain
  Canaries" (arXiv:2603.28013).

Type
  Research.

Audience
  Security specialists, SREs and engineers who deploy or red-team LLM agents. Also systems
  researchers who evaluate agent security.

Why is the topic important right now?
  Agents now read untrusted web pages, PDFs and tool output and hand content to other
  agents, but their robustness is reported as a single attack-success rate. Across 950 runs
  with five production LLMs, we found the following:
  - Pipelines with the same score stopped injections at different stages.
  - DeepSeek Chat resisted 0 of 24 injections in pre-seeded memory but executed 8 of 8 in
    tool results.
  - Three lightweight defenses failed on the channels they do not inspect.
  Operators can use the method today. It needs only a regular expression over tool
  traffic, and it works with closed models.

Non-text elements
  Four figures (PNG, 1400 px or wider): two pipelines with the same score but different
  kill chains; the benchmark setup; the last stage reached per model; attack success by
  model and channel. One short Python listing (about 20 lines).

Length
  About 1,850 words of body text plus the figures and the listing, roughly four to five
  ;login: pages.
